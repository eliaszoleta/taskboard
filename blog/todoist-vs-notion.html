Todoist: Fast, Focused, But Limited on the Free Plan

Todoist has been a go-to task manager since 2007. It does one thing and does it well: task management. You can add tasks using natural language ("submit report every Friday at 9am"), organize them into projects, assign priority levels, and track your productivity over time. The interface is clean and the mobile apps are among the best available.

The catch is the free plan. You're limited to 5 active projects, no kanban board view, and no reminders. For someone managing a handful of personal tasks, that might be enough. But if you want to organize work by visual columns — or simply want to grow your usage over time without hitting a paywall — Todoist's free tier starts to feel constrictive quickly.

Todoist Key Features

  • Natural language date input ("next Monday," "every weekday")
  • Four priority levels with color coding (P1–P4)
  • Recurring tasks with flexible schedules
  • Projects, sub-projects, labels, and filters
  • Productivity karma score and streak tracking
  • Integrations with Google Calendar, Slack, Zapier

✅ Todoist Pros

  • Excellent mobile apps

Notion: Powerful, But You'll Earn That Power

Notion is one of the most flexible productivity tools ever built. You can create anything with it: task databases, project wikis, personal journals, team handbooks, reading lists, and much more. The kanban board, table, calendar, and gallery views are all available — but only after you've set up a database yourself.

That flexibility is both Notion's biggest strength and its biggest weakness. For users who enjoy designing their own systems, Notion is genuinely exciting. For users who just want to track their tasks and get on with their day, Notion often becomes a time sink. It's easy to spend 45 minutes building the perfect task template and then not actually use it consistently.

Notion's free plan is generous (unlimited pages for personal use), but the learning curve is steep. If you've ever searched "Notion task management template" and ended up watching a 20-minute YouTube tutorial, you know what we mean.

Notion Key Features

  • Flexible page and database architecture
  • Multiple views: table, kanban, calendar, gallery, list
  • Relational databases with rollups and formulas
  • Rich text editing and slash commands
  • Team wikis and shared workspaces
  • Notion AI assistant (paid add-on)

✅ Notion Pros

  • Extremely flexible — build anything
  • Great for notes alongside tasks
  • Generous free plan for personal use
  • Beautiful, modern interface
  • Strong team collaboration tools

❌ Notion Cons

  • High learning curve
  • Requires setup before it's useful
  • Easy to over-engineer your system
  • No natural language date input
  • Mobile app is slower than alternatives
  • Offline mode is limited

Pricing: Free (personal use). Plus: $10/user/month. Business: $15/user/month.

The Simplicity Problem With Most Task Tools

There's a pattern that repeats itself constantly in the productivity community: someone discovers a powerful tool, spends days customizing it, gets excited by the possibilities — and then quietly stops using it two weeks later because maintaining the system requires more energy than just doing the work.

Productivity researchers call this "meta-work" — work about work. And both Todoist and Notion, when used to their full potential, require significant meta-work to keep running smoothly. You need to regularly groom your project list, update recurring tasks, tidy up your Notion databases, and maintain your labels and filters.

The tools that people actually stick with long-term are almost always the simpler ones. A board you open every morning and close every evening — one that shows you exactly what needs to happen today — beats a sophisticated system you abandon after a month.

This is the philosophy behind AchieverBoard: remove every barrier between you and getting organized. No setup, no account, no complexity. Just a board that works.
