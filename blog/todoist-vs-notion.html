Todoist vs Notion: Which Task Manager Is Right for You?

Todoist is laser-focused on tasks. Notion is a flexible all-in-one workspace. We compare both honestly so you can make the right call.

📅 March 15, 2026 ⏱ 9 min read 🏷 Comparison

Introduction: Two Very Different Tools

When people search "Todoist vs Notion," they're usually trying to solve a real problem: they've tried sticky notes, spreadsheets, and multiple apps, and they still haven't found something that sticks. Both Todoist and Notion are popular choices — but they're built on fundamentally different philosophies, and picking the wrong one can actually make your productivity worse, not better.

This comparison cuts through the marketing and looks at what each tool actually does well, where it falls short, and who each one is best suited for. We'll also look at some alternatives for people who find both tools more than they need.

Todoist Overview

Todoist has been around since 2007 and is one of the most polished task managers available. It does one thing — managing tasks — and it does it very well. You can add tasks using natural language ("submit report every Friday at 9am"), organize them into projects, set priority levels (P1 through P4), and track your productivity over time with the built-in karma score.

The interface is clean and fast. On mobile, it's arguably the best task app available. You can capture a thought in seconds, and the quick-add shortcut works from anywhere on your desktop.

Key Features

  • Natural language date parsing ("next Monday," "in 3 days")
  • Recurring tasks with flexible schedules
  • Four priority levels (P1–P4) with color coding
  • Project and sub-project organization
  • Labels and filters for cross-project views
  • Productivity tracking and streak-based karma system
  • Integrations with Google Calendar, Slack, Zapier, and more
  • Browser extension and email-to-task forwarding

✅ Todoist Pros

  • Fast, intuitive interface
  • Best-in-class mobile apps

❌ Todoist Cons

  • No kanban board on free plan

  • Limited customization

  • Business: $6/month/user (billed annually) — team features, admin controls

Notion Overview

Notion launched in 2016 and quickly became popular as a "second brain" tool — a place to store notes, write documents, build wikis, and yes, manage tasks. It's incredibly flexible. You can build almost anything with Notion: a personal CRM, a project tracker, a recipe database, or a full company knowledge base.

The flip side of that flexibility is complexity. Notion requires you to set up your own systems. Out of the box, it's essentially a blank canvas. If you want a task manager, you'll need to either build one yourself (using database views) or use a community template. This works great for people who enjoy building their own systems, but it's a barrier for anyone who wants to jump straight into organizing their tasks.

Key Features

  • Flexible page and database structure
  • Multiple views: table, board (kanban), calendar, gallery, list
  • Relational databases with rollups and formulas
  • Rich text editing with slash commands
  • Team wikis and shared workspaces
  • Template gallery (tasks, projects, goals)
  • AI writing assistant (Notion AI, paid add-on)
  • Integrations with Slack, GitHub, Jira, and more

✅ Notion Pros

  • Extremely flexible — build what you need
  • Excellent for note-taking alongside tasks
  • Great for team documentation
  • Generous free plan for personal use
  • Beautiful, modern interface
  • Strong database capabilities

❌ Notion Cons

  • High learning curve to get productive
  • No natural language date input
  • Can become cluttered and disorganized
  • Mobile app is slower than competitors
  • Offline mode is limited
  • Overkill for simple task management

Notion Pricing

  • Free: Unlimited pages and blocks for personal use, limited guest access
  • Plus: $10/month/user — unlimited guests, custom domains, 30-day history
  • Business: $15/month/user — advanced permissions, 90-day history, SAML SSO
  • Enterprise: Custom pricing

Head-to-Head: Key Comparisons

For Daily Task Management

If your main goal is to track your daily tasks, deadlines, and to-dos, Todoist wins. It's purpose-built for this. You can add tasks in seconds, schedule them with natural language, and get reminders automatically. Notion can do all this, but you'll spend significant time setting it up first — and then maintaining the system as your needs change.

For Teams and Collaboration

This is more nuanced. Todoist is perfectly adequate for shared projects among small teams. But Notion shines when your team also needs to write documentation, share meeting notes, or build a company wiki alongside their task tracking. If your team uses tasks and docs together, Notion is the better long-term investment.

For Simplicity

Todoist is simpler by design. You get a clean interface, clear priorities, and a fast workflow. Notion's power comes at the cost of simplicity — setting up a Notion workspace takes real effort, and it's easy to over-engineer your system and spend more time organizing than doing.

For Individuals on a Budget

Both have free plans. Todoist's free tier is more limited (5 projects), but it's perfectly usable for most individuals. Notion's free tier is more generous for personal use. However, if you want a completely free kanban board with no project limits, you might consider a simpler alternative.

Why Simplicity Matters More Than You Think

There's a growing trend of "productivity tool fatigue" — people spending more time maintaining their task system than actually completing tasks. Both Todoist and Notion, when used to their full extent, require regular maintenance: cleaning up completed tasks, reorganizing projects, updating labels, tweaking filters.

Research in behavioral psychology suggests that the more decisions a system requires, the more likely you are to avoid using it. A system you actually use — even an imperfect one — will always outperform a perfect system that you ignore.

This is why simpler tools continue to attract users despite the power of tools like Notion. A kanban board with a few columns, drag-and-drop cards, and clear due dates is often all most people need to stay organized and productive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Todoist better than Notion for task management?

For pure task management, Todoist is generally better. It's purpose-built for tasks with natural language input, priority levels, and recurring tasks. Notion is more powerful as an all-in-one workspace but requires significant setup to work as a task manager.

Is Todoist free?

Todoist offers a free plan limited to 5 active projects and basic features. Paid plans start at $4/month (billed annually) and unlock unlimited projects, reminders, and more advanced features.

Can Notion replace Todoist?

Notion can replace Todoist if you're willing to build a custom task database. However, it lacks Todoist's dedicated task features like natural language scheduling. For most users, Todoist is more practical for day-to-day task management.

What is the best free task management app?

Several apps offer strong free plans including Todoist (5 projects), Notion (unlimited personal pages), Trello (10 boards), and AchieverBoard (unlimited tasks, no sign-up required for solo use).

Which is better for teams — Todoist or Notion?

For teams that also create documentation and wikis alongside tasks, Notion is the better choice. For teams that need primarily task tracking and project management without the overhead, Todoist or Asana tend to be more straightforward.

Conclusion

Todoist and Notion are both excellent tools — but for very different people. Todoist is the right choice if you primarily need a reliable, fast task manager with great mobile support. Notion is the right choice if you want an all-in-one workspace that can handle tasks, notes, documentation, and team collaboration under one roof.

If you find yourself overwhelmed by both options — or if you've tried both and found them more complex than your workflow requires — it's worth considering whether a simpler, more focused tool might serve you better.
